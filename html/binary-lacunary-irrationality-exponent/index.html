The irrationality exponent of small-height binary lacunary series is 2

Ruiyi Zhang Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA
Abstract.

Let N≥2N\geq 2 be an integer and let λj\lambda_{j} be nonzero rational numbers with log⁡H⁢(λj)=o⁢(2j)\log H(\lambda_{j})=o(2^{j}), where H⁢(u/v)=max⁡(|u|,v)H(u/v)=\max(|u|,v) for u/vu/v in lowest terms with v>0v>0. We prove that ∑j≥0λj⁢N−2j\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}N^{-2^{j}} is irrational and has irrationality exponent 22. The proof uses Padé approximants to the tails ∑r≥0λk+r⁢z2r\sum_{r\geq 0}\lambda_{k+r}z^{2^{r}}. The two Hankel families they need satisfy parity recursions, so they are nonzero for all nonzero weights. After the finite prefix of the series is cleared, the approximants of order nn give integer linear forms with height N(n+1/2)⁢2kN^{(n+1/2)2^{k}} and error N−(n+1/2)⁢2kN^{-(n+1/2)2^{k}}, up to factors eo⁢(2k)e^{o(2^{k})}. Only finitely many orders are used for each target exponent. The height condition cannot be weakened to log⁡H⁢(λj)≤C⁢2j\log H(\lambda_{j})\leq C2^{j} for any constant C>0C>0: for every CC some such weights give exponent greater than 22, and for larger CC positive integer weights can give a rational number or a Liouville number.

1. Introduction

For an irrational real number ξ\xi, its irrationality exponent is

μ⁢(ξ)=sup{ν∈ℝ:|ξ−pq|<q−ν⁢ for infinitely many ⁢(p,q)∈ℤ×ℤ>0}.\mu(\xi)=\sup\left\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:\left|\xi-\frac{p}{q}\right|<q^{-\nu}% \text{ for infinitely many }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\right\}.

The pigeonhole principle gives μ⁢(ξ)≥2\mu(\xi)\geq 2 (see the end of Section 5). Write L=log⁡NL=\log N. For a nonzero rational number in lowest terms, put

(1.1) H⁢(u/v)=max⁡(|u|,v),u∈ℤ∖{0},v∈ℤ>0,gcd⁡(u,v)=1.H(u/v)=\max(|u|,v),\qquad u\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\quad v\in\mathbb{Z}_{>% 0},\quad\gcd(u,v)=1.

We show that equality holds for binary lacunary series with rational weights of sufficiently small height.

Theorem 1.

Let N∈ℤN\in\mathbb{Z}, N≥2N\geq 2, and let (λj)j≥0(\lambda_{j})_{j\geq 0} be a sequence in ℚ∖{0}\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\} such that log⁡H⁢(λj)=o⁢(2j)\log H(\lambda_{j})=o(2^{j}). Then the absolutely convergent series

(1.2) x=∑j=0∞λj⁢N−2j=λ0N+λ1N2+λ2N4+λ3N8+⋯x=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}N^{-2^{j}}=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{N}+\frac{\lambda% _{1}}{N^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{N^{4}}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{N^{8}}+\cdots

is irrational and satisfies μ⁢(x)=2\mu(x)=2.

Geometric weights λj=λj\lambda_{j}=\lambda^{j}, with λ∈ℚ∖{0}\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}, satisfy the hypothesis because log⁡H⁢(λj)=O⁢(j)\log H(\lambda^{j})=O(j). Every sequence drawn in arbitrary order from a finite subset of ℚ∖{0}\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\} also satisfies the hypothesis, since its heights are bounded.

Duverney’s criterion [3, Corollary 3.4] already gives irrationality under this height condition. For wj=N2jw_{j}=N^{2^{j}}, its rationality exception would require

λj⁢λj+2=wj⁢λj+12\lambda_{j}\lambda_{j+2}=w_{j}\lambda_{j+1}^{2}

eventually. Here log⁡|λj⁢λj+2/λj+12|=o⁢(2j)\log|\lambda_{j}\lambda_{j+2}/\lambda_{j+1}^{2}|=o(2^{j}), whereas log⁡wj=2j⁢L\log w_{j}=2^{j}L, so the exception cannot occur. Duverney [3, Theorem 3.2] also studies finite exponent bounds. For related work, see his later paper [4].

Duverney, Kurosawa and Shiokawa [5, Theorem 1] consider signed series ∑ϵj/xj\sum\epsilon_{j}/x_{j}, where xj>1x_{j}>1 are rational and xj+1≥xj2x_{j+1}\geq x_{j}^{2} eventually. Their denominator condition is

(1.3) δ1=den⁡(x1),δj+1=δj2⁢den⁡(xj+1/xj2),log⁡δj+1=o⁢(log⁡xj),\delta_{1}=\operatorname{den}(x_{1}),\qquad\delta_{j+1}=\delta_{j}^{2}% \operatorname{den}(x_{j+1}/x_{j}^{2}),\qquad\log\delta_{j+1}=o(\log x_{j}),

where den\operatorname{den} is the reduced positive denominator. They conclude μ⁢(∑ϵj/xj)=lim supjlog⁡xj+1/log⁡xj\mu(\sum\epsilon_{j}/x_{j})=\limsup_{j}\log x_{j+1}/\log x_{j}. For xj=N2j/(t⁢|λj|)x_{j}=N^{2^{j}}/(t|\lambda_{j}|) with a fixed rational t>0t>0, the ratio is t⁢|λj|2/|λj+1|t|\lambda_{j}|^{2}/|\lambda_{j+1}|; the accumulated condition in (1.3) can fail even for a finite set of weights.

Amou and Bugeaud [1, Lemma 3] give an earlier criterion for ∑Bj/Aj\sum B_{j}/A_{j}, with strictly increasing positive integers AjA_{j} and nonzero integers BjB_{j}. Their hypotheses include ratios Sj=(Aj+1/|Bj+1|)/(Aj/|Bj|)2≥2S_{j}=(A_{j+1}/|B_{j+1}|)/(A_{j}/|B_{j}|)^{2}\geq 2 eventually and ∏r=0jdr2j−r=Ajo⁢(1)\prod_{r=0}^{j}d_{r}^{2^{j-r}}=A_{j}^{o(1)}, where dj=den⁡(Sj)d_{j}=\operatorname{den}(S_{j}) and d0=|B1|d_{0}=|B_{1}|; small heights alone do not imply this cumulative arithmetic condition.

For unit weights, Shallit [8, Theorems 8–9] proved bounded partial quotients when N≥3N\geq 3. For N=2N=2, he states the corresponding result for 2⁢∑j≥02−2j2\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-2^{j}} [8, Theorem 11 and following example]. He also states a recursion for weights (−1)j(-1)^{j} when N≥3N\geq 3 [8, Theorem 12].

For nonzero integral geometric weights, Bugeaud, Han, Wen and Yao [2, Theorem 2.1] give exponent 22: g⁢(z)=∑r≥0λr⁢z2r−1g(z)=\sum_{r\geq 0}\lambda^{r}z^{2^{r}-1} has integer coefficients, satisfies g⁢(z)=1+λ⁢z⁢g⁢(z2)g(z)=1+\lambda zg(z^{2}), and has nonzero Hankel determinants of every order by Lemma 2.1. The multiplier λ⁢z\lambda z is nonzero on the orbit of 1/N1/N. The same theorem covers periodic rational weights. If the period is p≥1p\geq 1 and Dper∈ℤ>0D_{\mathrm{per}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0} clears the denominators, then f⁢(z)=Dper⁢∑j≥0λj⁢z2j−1∈ℤ⁢[[z]]f(z)=D_{\mathrm{per}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}z^{2^{j}-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z]] converges in the unit disk and satisfies f⁢(z)=P⁢(z)+z2p−1⁢f⁢(z2p)f(z)=P(z)+z^{2^{p}-1}f(z^{2^{p}}) with P∈ℤ⁢[z]P\in\mathbb{Z}[z]. Lemma 2.1 gives every Hankel order, and z2p−1z^{2^{p}-1} does not vanish on the orbit of 1/N1/N. Since x=f⁢(1/N)/(Dper⁢N)x=f(1/N)/(D_{\mathrm{per}}N), [2, Theorem 2.1] gives μ⁢(x)=2\mu(x)=2. Su [9, Theorem 7.3] gives a formal continued fraction for ∑j≥0cj⁢z2j−1\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{j}z^{2^{j}-1} with c0=1c_{0}=1 and nonzero cjc_{j} in any field.

Rajchert [7] and Su [9] study related questions on irrationality exponents and continued fractions of Mahler-type series. None of our results depends on these works; apart from standard facts, every step is proved here.

For geometric weights, Mahler’s method also gives transcendence [6, Corollary 2]. The series F⁢(z)=∑r≥0λr⁢z2rF(z)=\sum_{r\geq 0}\lambda^{r}z^{2^{r}} has radius 11 and Hadamard gaps, so it is transcendental over ℂ⁢(z)\mathbb{C}(z). It satisfies F⁢(z)=z+λ⁢F⁢(z2)F(z)=z+\lambda F(z^{2}); the matrix of the system for (1,F⁢(z))𝖳(1,F(z))^{\mathsf{T}} and its inverse have polynomial entries. The orbit of 1/N1/N therefore contains no singular point.

Our proof uses moving tails and their Hankel recursions. After clearing the finite prefix, adjacent Padé approximants give integer pairs with nonzero determinant. For each target exponent the finite set of orders is fixed first, so all coefficient costs are o⁢(2k)o(2^{k}) at tail shift kk. Section 6 shows that the height scale is sharp.

Throughout the proof write

(1.4) λj=ujvj,uj∈ℤ∖{0},vj∈ℤ>0,gcd⁡(uj,vj)=1,hj=log⁡H⁢(λj).\begin{gathered}\lambda_{j}=\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}},\qquad u_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}% \setminus\{0\},\quad v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\quad\gcd(u_{j},v_{j})=1,\\ h_{j}=\log H(\lambda_{j}).\end{gathered}

In particular,

(1.5) e−hj≤|λj|≤ehj.e^{-h_{j}}\leq|\lambda_{j}|\leq e^{h_{j}}.

The upper bound and hj=o⁢(2j)h_{j}=o(2^{j}) give absolute convergence of (1.2). The moving tails are

(1.6) Fk⁢(z)=∑r≥0λk+r⁢z2r,Fk⁢(z)=λk⁢z+Fk+1⁢(z2).F_{k}(z)=\sum_{r\geq 0}\lambda_{k+r}z^{2^{r}},\qquad F_{k}(z)=\lambda_{k}z+F_{% k+1}(z^{2}).

For each fixed kk, the series converges absolutely for |z|<1|z|<1.

5. Proof of Theorem 1

First fix n=1n=1 and let k→∞k\to\infty. By (4.6) and (4.11), the forms B1,k⁢x−A1,kB_{1,k}x-A_{1,k} are eventually nonzero and tend to zero. If x=a/bx=a/b were rational, with a∈ℤa\in\mathbb{Z} and b∈ℤ>0b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}, then

would be a nonzero integer tending to zero, a contradiction. This proves irrationality.

For the upper bound we use a fixed finite set of orders. Fix an integer M≥1M\geq 1 and use primary orders M≤n≤2⁢MM\leq n\leq 2M, each with companion n+1n+1. The whole order set is therefore

(5.1) ℬ={M,M+1,…,2⁢M+1}.\mathcal{B}=\{M,M+1,\ldots,2M+1\}.

Put

(5.2) αM=1+1M+1/2.\alpha_{M}=1+\frac{1}{M+1/2}.

Order the primary scales τn,k\tau_{n,k} by increasing kk, and then increasing nn. This is their numerical order, because the largest scale in one block is (2⁢M+12)⁢L⁢2k(2M+\tfrac{1}{2})L2^{k}, and the first in the next is (2⁢M+1)⁢L⁢2k(2M+1)L2^{k}. Within a block, successive ratios are at most

M+3/2M+1/2=αM.\frac{M+3/2}{M+1/2}=\alpha_{M}.

At a block transition the ratio is

2⁢(M+1/2)2⁢M+1/2<αM.\frac{2(M+1/2)}{2M+1/2}<\alpha_{M}.

The companion scale also satisfies τn+1,k≤αM⁢τn,k\tau_{n+1,k}\leq\alpha_{M}\tau_{n,k}.

Fix 0<η<10<\eta<1. Since the order set ℬ\mathcal{B} is finite, (4.10) and (4.11) hold beyond one common threshold. For each sufficiently large primary scale τ=τn,k\tau=\tau_{n,k}, its two rows i=n,n+1i=n,n+1 satisfy

(5.3) 0<Bi,k≤exp⁡((αM+η)⁢τ),|Bi,k⁢x−Ai,k|≤exp⁡(−(1−η)⁢τ).0<B_{i,k}\leq\exp((\alpha_{M}+\eta)\tau),\qquad|B_{i,k}x-A_{i,k}|\leq\exp(-(1-% \eta)\tau).

6. Sharpness of the height scale

The little-oo condition cannot be replaced by a bound log⁡H⁢(λj)≤C∗⁢2j\log H(\lambda_{j})\leq C_{*}2^{j} with any constant C∗>0C_{*}>0. Below, small coefficients give exponent greater than 22, and the coefficient 12⁢L\tfrac{1}{2}L gives both a rational and a Liouville sum.

Fix 0<θ<1/30<\theta<1/3, and put

(6.1) mj=⌊θ⁢2j⌋,λj=N(−1)j⁢mj,ej=2j−(−1)j⁢mj.m_{j}=\lfloor\theta 2^{j}\rfloor,\qquad\lambda_{j}=N^{(-1)^{j}m_{j}},\qquad e_% {j}=2^{j}-(-1)^{j}m_{j}.

The weights are positive rationals, with

(6.2) log⁡H⁢(λj)=mj⁢L=θ⁢L⁢2j+O⁢(1).\log H(\lambda_{j})=m_{j}L=\theta L2^{j}+O(1).

The associated sum has the exact digit expansion

x=∑j≥0N−ej.x=\sum_{j\geq 0}N^{-e_{j}}.

The exponents are strictly increasing: for odd jj,

ej+1−ej=2j−mj−mj+1≥(1−3⁢θ)⁢2j>0,e_{j+1}-e_{j}=2^{j}-m_{j}-m_{j+1}\geq(1-3\theta)2^{j}>0,

and for even jj their difference is 2j+mj+mj+1>02^{j}+m_{j}+m_{j+1}>0. The gaps tend to infinity, so the expansion is not eventually periodic and xx is irrational.

Let pj/qjp_{j}/q_{j} be the truncation through index jj, in lowest terms. Its denominator is exactly qj=Nejq_{j}=N^{e_{j}}, because its numerator is 11 modulo NN and hence is coprime to every prime dividing NN. The increasing integer exponents give

(6.3) 0<x−pjqj≤N−ej+11−N−1≤2⁢N−ej+1.0<x-\frac{p_{j}}{q_{j}}\leq\frac{N^{-e_{j+1}}}{1-N^{-1}}\leq 2N^{-e_{j+1}}.

Along even indices,

ej+1ej⟶2⁢(1+θ)1−θ>2.\frac{e_{j+1}}{e_{j}}\longrightarrow\frac{2(1+\theta)}{1-\theta}>2.

For every ν<2⁢(1+θ)/(1−θ)\nu<2(1+\theta)/(1-\theta), (6.3) is less than qj−νq_{j}^{-\nu} at all sufficiently large even indices. Consequently

(6.4) μ⁢(x)≥2⁢(1+θ)1−θ>2.\mu(x)\geq\frac{2(1+\theta)}{1-\theta}>2.

Equivalently, for each 0<κ<L/30<\kappa<L/3 this gives

log⁡H⁢(λj)∼κ⁢2j,μ⁢(x)≥2⁢(L+κ)L−κ.\log H(\lambda_{j})\sim\kappa 2^{j},\qquad\mu(x)\geq\frac{2(L+\kappa)}{L-% \kappa}.

Given any C∗>0C_{*}>0, choose 0<κ<min⁡(C∗,L/3)0<\kappa<\min(C_{*},L/3). Then the same weights satisfy log⁡H⁢(λj)≤C∗⁢2j\log H(\lambda_{j})\leq C_{*}2^{j} for all jj, but their sum has exponent greater than 22.

A rational sum is possible with positive integer weights. Set

(6.5) λ0=N−1,λj=N2j−1−1(j≥1).\lambda_{0}=N-1,\qquad\lambda_{j}=N^{2^{j-1}}-1\quad(j\geq 1).

For j≥1j\geq 1,

λj⁢N−2j=N−2j−1−N−2j,\lambda_{j}N^{-2^{j}}=N^{-2^{j-1}}-N^{-2^{j}},

so the partial sums telescope:

∑j=0mλj⁢N−2j=1−N−2m.\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda_{j}N^{-2^{j}}=1-N^{-2^{m}}.

The infinite sum is therefore 11, whereas

(6.6) log⁡H⁢(λj)∼12⁢L⁢ 2j.\log H(\lambda_{j})\sim\frac{1}{2}L\,2^{j}.

The same coefficient also allows a Liouville number. Keep λ0=N−1\lambda_{0}=N-1, and replace the later weights by

λ~j=N2j−1−1+𝟏{j=r2⁢ for some ⁢r≥1}(j≥1).\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}=N^{2^{j-1}}-1+\boldsymbol{1}_{\{j=r^{2}\text{ for some% }r\geq 1\}}\quad(j\geq 1).

These are again positive integers and satisfy (6.6). Their sum is

x~=1+ξN,ξN=∑r≥1N−2r2.\widetilde{x}=1+\xi_{N},\qquad\xi_{N}=\sum_{r\geq 1}N^{-2^{r^{2}}}.

Its digit expansion is not eventually periodic, so ξN\xi_{N} is irrational. The truncation through r=Rr=R has reduced denominator qR=N2R2q_{R}=N^{2^{R^{2}}}, since its numerator is 11 modulo NN, and

0<ξN−∑r=1RN−2r2≤2⁢N−2(R+1)2=2⁢qR−22⁢R+1.0<\xi_{N}-\sum_{r=1}^{R}N^{-2^{r^{2}}}\leq 2N^{-2^{(R+1)^{2}}}=2q_{R}^{-2^{2R+% 1}}.

The same bound holds after adding 11. Since 22⁢R+1→∞2^{2R+1}\to\infty, this proves μ⁢(x~)=∞\mu(\widetilde{x})=\infty.

The height condition is not necessary for an individual series. For example, given an integer b≥2b\geq 2, the weights λj=b−2j\lambda_{j}=b^{-2^{j}} have log⁡H⁢(λj)=(log⁡b)⁢2j\log H(\lambda_{j})=(\log b)2^{j}, but their sum equals ∑j≥0(N⁢b)−2j\sum_{j\geq 0}(Nb)^{-2^{j}}. Theorem 1, applied with base N⁢bNb and constant weights 11, gives exponent 22 for that sum.
