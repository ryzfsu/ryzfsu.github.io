Irrationality of Li₂(1/2): Lean proof map✓ Lean 4 verified

Ruiyi Zhang · Lean 4.35.0-rc4 · Mathlib 25e572b8

Paper Lean source (v2) DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23251312

The paper proves that Li₂(1/2) = Σn≥1 2−n/n² is irrational using determinant polynomials Gm(T), an analytic bound by a logarithmic energy, a common denominator from binomial valuations, and nonvanishing from a Lucas congruence modulo 83. The Lean 4 formalization proves the same statement with no hypotheses. This page shows its dependency graph, extracted from the compiled Lean environment: every project declaration that the main theorem uses, directly or indirectly, with the axioms each one depends on.

Statement

In the paper

For |w| ≤ 1 let Li₂(w) = Σn≥1 wn/n².

Theorem 1. The number Li₂(1/2) is irrational.

In Lean (Solution.lean)

theorem li2_half_irrational :
    Irrational
      (∑' k : ℕ, ((2 : ℝ)⁻¹) ^ (k + 1)
                    / ((k : ℝ) + 1) ^ 2)

The index k starts at 0, so k + 1 runs over n ≥ 1. Irrational is Mathlib's predicate x ∉ range((↑) : ℚ → ℝ).

Axioms



These three are Lean's standard axioms (propositional extensionality, choice, quotient soundness). No sorryAx and no project-specific axiom occurs. The released package also records acceptance by the Lean kernel through the comparator and by the independent nanoda type checker.



How the parts are formed


The paper derives Theorem 1 from three estimates: the analytic bound (1.2), integerization (1.3) and nonvanishing (1.4); §10 combines them. The Lean final theorem li2_half_irrational_of_envelope_v2 has the same shape: it applies an endpoint criterion, which formalizes the §10 argument, to the three estimates. The map takes the dependency closure of the top-level Lean declarations of each estimate and of the endpoint criterion. Parts are assigned to declarations, not files: a declaration reached from exactly one of them belongs to it; a declaration reached from two or more is a shared definition; the few declarations above all of them are the final wiring and belong to the Final argument. A file can hold declarations of several parts; the module view marks such files. Certificate data and vendored code are a separate axis: they are classified by content (generated tables and their kernel checks, third-party sources), and each such box is used by exactly one estimate.


By construction an estimate never uses a declaration of another estimate: whatever it uses lies in its own closure, so it is either its own or shared. That is a consequence of the rule, not a check. The checks below test the extracted graph.
