Equal radicals of consecutive products

Ruiyi Zhang Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA
Abstract.

For every fixed k≥2k\geq 2 and ε>0\varepsilon>0, Fk,k⁢(X)≪k,εX2/3+εF_{k,k}(X)\ll_{k,\varepsilon}X^{2/3+\varepsilon}. For k≥3k\geq 3 this improves the X1+o⁢(1)X^{1+o(1)} bound of Lebowitz–Lockard. For k=2k=2, Kane’s localized estimates give the large-quotient endpoint bound. Our independent proof combines a Pell bound for partner multiplicities, a quotient-tail estimate, and Farey spacing for small quotients. Reuss’s determinant estimate also gives N⁢(H,Y)≪εHε⁢(Y/H+H7/12)N(H,Y)\ll_{\varepsilon}\allowbreak H^{\varepsilon}(Y/H+H^{7/12}), uniformly for H≥2H\geq 2 and 1≤Y<H21\leq Y<H^{2}, where N⁢(H,Y)N(H,Y) counts integers t∈(H,2⁢H]t\in(H,2H] with rad⁡(t⁢(t+1))≤Y\operatorname{rad}(t(t+1))\leq Y. A cubic family gives F⁢(X)≥(41/3/3)⁢X1/3−O⁢(1)F(X)\geq(4^{1/3}/3)X^{1/3}-O(1).

1. Introduction

For a positive integer aa, let rad⁡(a)=∏p∣ap\operatorname{rad}(a)=\prod_{p\mid a}p. We study the ordered count

F⁢(X)=#⁢{1≤m<n≤X:rad⁡(m⁢(m+1))=rad⁡(n⁢(n+1))}.F(X)=\#\{1\leq m<n\leq X:\operatorname{rad}(m(m+1))=\operatorname{rad}(n(n+1))\}.

More generally, put

Pk⁢(t)=∏j=0k−1(t+j),Fk,k⁢(X)=#⁢{1≤m<n≤X:rad⁡(Pk⁢(m))=rad⁡(Pk⁢(n))}.P_{k}(t)=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(t+j),\qquad F_{k,k}(X)=\#\{1\leq m<n\leq X:% \operatorname{rad}(P_{k}(m))=\operatorname{rad}(P_{k}(n))\}.

Thus F⁢(X)=F2,2⁢(X)F(X)=F_{2,2}(X). We impose no disjointness condition on the two blocks, and all implied constants may depend on the fixed integer kk.

Theorem 1.

For every fixed integer k≥2k\geq 2, every ε>0\varepsilon>0, and every real X≥2X\geq 2,

Fk,k⁢(X)≪k,εX2/3+ε.F_{k,k}(X)\ll_{k,\varepsilon}X^{2/3+\varepsilon}.

Erdős asked for the size of F⁢(X)F(X) [3]. Balasubramanian, Shorey and Waldschmidt [1, §5] studied non-overlapping blocks of consecutive integers with the same prime divisors. Lebowitz–Lockard [7, Theorems 5 and 10] proved Fk,k⁢(X)≤X1+o⁢(1)F_{k,k}(X)\leq X^{1+o(1)}. For k≥3k\geq 3, Theorem 1 replaces this exponent by 2/3+ε2/3+\varepsilon. Pell equations of this kind go back to Lehmer’s work on Størmer’s problem [8]; see [1, §2].

For k=2k=2, Kane’s localized estimates [6, Propositions 7 and 10] (Propositions 6 and 9 of [5]) give the large-quotient endpoint bound after specialization to (1,t,−(t+1))(1,t,-(t+1)) and dyadic summation. In the proof below, Lemma 2.4 handles the small-quotient range; together with the endpoint-fibre and quotient-tail bounds, it gives an independent proof of the k=2k=2 case.

Put qk⁢(n)=Pk⁢(n)/rad⁡(Pk⁢(n))q_{k}(n)=P_{k}(n)/\operatorname{rad}(P_{k}(n)). Splitting at a cutoff KK and applying the endpoint-fibre, quotient-tail and small-quotient estimates gives, for max⁡{k,2}≤K≤X\max\{k,2\}\leq K\leq X,

(1.1) Fk,k⁢(X)≪k,εXε⁢(K2+X/K+X1/2).F_{k,k}(X)\ll_{k,\varepsilon}X^{\varepsilon}\bigl{(}K^{2}+X/K+X^{1/2}\bigr{)}.

For X≥k3X\geq k^{3}, taking K=X1/3K=X^{1/3} in (1.1) proves Theorem 1. The bounded range 2≤X<k32\leq X<k^{3} is absorbed into the implied constant, since kk is fixed. Theorem 2 gives an explicit lower-bound family for Erdős’s count. Theorem 3 follows from known estimates; its deduction appears in Appendix A. Section 6 gives two consequences of the pair bound. The tools used for numerical checks and literature searches are identified at the end of the paper.

3. A cubic lower-bound family

Theorem 2.

There is an absolute constant CC such that, for every real X≥2X\geq 2,

F⁢(X)≥41/33⁢X1/3−C.F(X)\geq\frac{4^{1/3}}{3}X^{1/3}-C.
Proof.

Erdős posed the product-support counting problem in [3]. We know of no published source for this particular polynomial family. Take integers t≥7t\geq 7 satisfying

t≢0(mod4),t≡1,2,7,8(mod9),t\not\equiv 0\pmod{4},\qquad t\equiv 1,2,7,8\pmod{9},

and put

m=t2−43,n=t3−3⁢t−24.m=\frac{t^{2}-4}{3},\qquad n=\frac{t^{3}-3t-2}{4}.

The resulting integers are positive, and

12⁢(n−m)=3⁢t3−4⁢t2−9⁢t+10>012(n-m)=3t^{3}-4t^{2}-9t+10>0

for t≥7t\geq 7, so m<nm<n. Direct multiplication gives

m⁢(m+1)=(t2−4)⁢(t2−1)9,n⁢(n+1)=(t2−4)⁢(t2−1)216.m(m+1)=\frac{(t^{2}-4)(t^{2}-1)}{9},\qquad n(n+1)=\frac{(t^{2}-4)(t^{2}-1)^{2}% }{16}.

For every prime other than 22 and 33, the two products have the same prime support. If t≡±1(mod9)t\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}, then 9∣t2−19\mid t^{2}-1 and 3∣t2−43\mid t^{2}-4; if t≡±2(mod9)t\equiv\pm 2\pmod{9}, then 9∣t2−49\mid t^{2}-4 and 3∣t2−13\mid t^{2}-1. Thus division by 99 leaves a factor 33 in the first product, and the second product is also divisible by 33.

If tt is odd, then 8∣t2−18\mid t^{2}-1, so both products remain even after their displayed divisions. If t≡2(mod4)t\equiv 2\pmod{4}, then 32∣t2−432\mid t^{2}-4, which again leaves both products even after division by 1616. Hence the two products have equal radicals.

The conditions specify twelve residue classes modulo 3636, of density 1/31/3. Since n=t3/4+O⁢(t)n=t^{3}/4+O(t), the number of such tt with n≤Xn\leq X is (41/3/3)⁢X1/3+O⁢(1)(4^{1/3}/3)X^{1/3}+O(1). The map t↦mt\mapsto m is strictly increasing for t≥7t\geq 7, so these give distinct pairs counted by F⁢(X)F(X). ∎

4. A radical-threshold estimate from known results

For real H≥2H\geq 2 and 1≤Y<H21\leq Y<H^{2}, define

N⁢(H,Y)=#⁢{t∈ℤ:H<t≤2⁢H,rad⁡(t⁢(t+1))≤Y}.N(H,Y)=\#\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:H<t\leq 2H,\operatorname{rad}(t(t+1))\leq Y\}.
Theorem 3.

For every ε>0\varepsilon>0,

N⁢(H,Y)≪εHε⁢(YH+H7/12)N(H,Y)\ll_{\varepsilon}H^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Y}{H}+H^{7/12}\right)
