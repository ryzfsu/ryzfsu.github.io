Irrationality of the trilogarithm at reciprocals of large integers

Ruiyi Zhang Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA
Abstract.

We prove that Li3⁡(1/a)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1/a) is irrational for every integer a≥67a\geq 67 and every integer a≤−70a\leq-70. Earlier ranges are |a|≥1038|a|\geq 1038 (Hata), a≥1173a\geq 1173 (Panzone), and a≥486a\geq 486, a≤−471a\leq-471 (Marcovecchio). For 4∣m4\mid m we form an m×mm\times m determinant. Its entries are linear forms in 11 and Li3⁡(1/a)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1/a). The kernel has only triple poles, so no other polylogarithm enters. We bound a common denominator of the coefficients. The determinant does not vanish along a prime-power subsequence that depends on aa. A logarithmic energy bounds its size. Exact density tables with directed rounding give the energy bounds at a=67a=67 and a=−70a=-70. A slope comparison extends them to all a≥67a\geq 67 and all a≤−70a\leq-70.

1. Introduction

For |z|<1|z|<1, define

Li3⁡(z)=∑n≥1znn3.\operatorname{Li}_{3}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{3}}.

Hata [3] constructed explicit rational approximations to polylogarithms at 1/q1/q. By Marcovecchio [5, p. 229], Hata’s method [3, Corollary 2.2] gives the linear independence over ℚ\mathbb{Q} of

1,Li1⁡(1/a),Li2⁡(1/a),Li3⁡(1/a)1,\ \operatorname{Li}_{1}(1/a),\ \operatorname{Li}_{2}(1/a),\ \operatorname{Li% }_{3}(1/a)

for every integer aa with |a|≥1038|a|\geq 1038. Panzone [6, Theorem 2, p. 108] gave a short proof that Li3⁡(1/d)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1/d) is irrational for every integer d≥1173d\geq 1173. He attributes this result to Hata. Marcovecchio [5, Theorem 7.1, p. 228; p. 229] extended the linear independence to a≥486a\geq 486 and a≤−471a\leq-471. In particular Li3⁡(1/a)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1/a) is irrational in these ranges.

Theorem 1.

For every integer a≥67a\geq 67 and every integer a≤−70a\leq-70, Li3⁡(1/a)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1/a) is irrational.

Theorem 1 concerns Li3⁡(1/a)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1/a) alone. In their ranges, the results of Hata and Marcovecchio give the stronger linear independence of four numbers.

The method is the determinant method of [8] for the dilogarithm, carried to weight three. We use only the determinant criterion of [8, Section 3]; Fact 1 restates it with proof. The analytic estimate uses Andreief’s identity [1], Cauchy’s double alternant [4], logarithmic energy [7], and the prime number theorem [2].
