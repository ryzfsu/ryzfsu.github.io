A subexponential upper bound for log c in the abc conjecture

Ruiyi Zhang Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA
(Date: November 20, 2026)
Abstract.

Let a,b,ca,b,c be positive integers with a+b=ca+b=c and gcd⁡(a,b)=1\gcd(a,b)=1. We prove the bound logc≤Cεrad(abc)ε\log c\leq C_{\varepsilon}\operatorname{rad}(abc)^{\varepsilon} for every ε>0\varepsilon>0. Under the contrary height assumption, a prime power dividing one side forces a nonzero polynomial to vanish on part of an integer simplex. A second prime splits that set into residue classes. Finite differences and Lucas’s congruence show that its points determine every polynomial up to the required degree.

Key words and phrases:
abc conjecture, radical, interpolation determinant, exponential sum over primes

1. Introduction

Write R=rad⁡(a⁢b⁢c)=∏p∣a⁢b⁢cpR=\operatorname{rad}(abc)=\prod_{p\mid abc}p and H=log⁡cH=\log c. The a⁢b⁢cabc conjecture predicts H≤(1+ε)⁢log⁡R+Oε⁢(1)H\leq(1+\varepsilon)\log R+O_{\varepsilon}(1) for every coprime positive solution of a+b=ca+b=c. It remains open. Stewart and Yu obtained H≪R1/3⁢(log⁡R)3H\ll R^{1/3}(\log R)^{3} [6, Theorem 1]. Pasten and Sepúlveda-Manzo obtained subexponential estimates under size conditions on the smaller summand [4, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]. Zhang gives H≪R1/3⁢exp⁡(−log⁡R/(3000⁢log⁡log⁡R))H\ll R^{1/3}\exp(-\log R/(3000\log\log R)) for all sufficiently large RR [7, Theorem 1] and subsequently obtains H≪εR2/9+εH\ll_{\varepsilon}R^{2/9+\varepsilon} [8, Theorem 1.1].

Theorem 1.1.

Let ε>0\varepsilon>0. There is a constant Cε>0C_{\varepsilon}>0 such that

logc≤Cεrad(abc)ε\log c\leq C_{\varepsilon}\operatorname{rad}(abc)^{\varepsilon}

for all coprime positive integers a,ba,b and c=a+bc=a+b.

Fix e=min⁡(ε,1/10)e=\min(\varepsilon,1/10) and suppose H≥ReH\geq R^{e}. For large RR, the side of least radical contains a prime power of logarithm >2⁢e⁢H>2eH. The ratio of the other sides is then close to a sign at that prime. Adjoining auxiliary primes gives a lattice of principal units with large first minimum. Two determinant estimates force a nonzero polynomial to vanish on a slice of an integer simplex.

Write λ\lambda for the slope of the slice. The first minimum excludes small denominators for its Fourier frequencies. Steuding and Technau’s prime estimate locates a prime qq with q⁢λq\lambda close to (0,1/m,…,(m−1)/m)(0,1/m,\ldots,(m-1)/m) [5, Theorem 3]. In each residue class modulo qq, the slice thus contains consecutive colors of a smaller simplex. Finite differences give interpolation on those colors. Lucas’s congruence lifts interpolation to the full slice, contradicting the vanishing polynomial.

The use of AI tools is described at the end of the paper.
