Abstract.

Let a+b=ca+b=c be pairwise coprime positive integers. Then, we introduce R=rad⁡(a⁢b⁢c)R=\operatorname{rad}(abc) and P⁢(a⁢b⁢c)=∏p∣a⁢b⁢cvp⁢(a⁢b⁢c)P(abc)=\prod_{p\mid abc}v_{p}(abc). We present here the proof of

P⁢(a⁢b⁢c),𝐝⁢(a⁢b⁢c)≪εR5/3+ε,P(abc),\ \mathbf{d}(abc)\ll_{\varepsilon}R^{5/3+\varepsilon},

where 𝐝\mathbf{d} is taken to be the divisor function. The first step in the deduction gives an aggregate fixed-radius estimate for CM points on an indefinite Shimura curve. In the case where the CM discriminants lie in [Δ,2⁢Δ][\Delta,2\Delta], the number of nearby ordered pairs is

#⁢{(P,Q)∈𝒫Δ2:d⁢(P,Q)≤r0}≪|𝒫Δ|+Δ⁢(1+Δ2N)⁢Nε,\#\{(P,Q)\in\mathscr{P}_{\Delta}^{2}:d(P,Q)\leq r_{0}\}\ll|\mathscr{P}_{\Delta% }|+\Delta\left(1+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{N}\right)N^{\varepsilon},

which holds uniformly in all polynomial ranges Δ≤NA\Delta\leq N^{A}. This is established by an elementary local orbit count in the Bruhat–Tits tree, using the characteristic-matrix framework of Bruinier, Li and Möller. The spatial Cauchy–Schwarz then yields a bound for the first absolute moment of the CM-packet. The ordinary AM–GM now applies directly to the logarithmic average in Pasten’s Arakelov estimate to complete the argument, and neither a fourth moment nor a pointwise subconvexity estimate is required.

1. Introduction

Let a,b,ca,b,c be pairwise coprime positive integers satisfying a+b=ca+b=c. Here

R=rad⁡(a⁢b⁢c),P⁢(a⁢b⁢c)=∏p∣a⁢b⁢cvp⁢(a⁢b⁢c),𝐝⁢(n)=∏p∣n(vp⁢(n)+1).R=\operatorname{rad}(abc),\qquad P(abc)=\prod_{p\mid abc}v_{p}(abc),\qquad% \mathbf{d}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl{(}v_{p}(n)+1\bigr{)}.

Using a comparison between classical and quaternionic modular degrees, Pasten was able to derive the unconditional bounds P⁢(a⁢b⁢c),𝐝⁢(a⁢b⁢c)≪εR8/3+εP(abc),\mathbf{d}(abc)\ll_{\varepsilon}R^{8/3+\varepsilon} by evaluating an integral differential at CM points [12, Theorem 1.11 and Theorem 16.8]. Our main result is a reduction of the exponent.

Rickards has established precise formulas regarding the intersections of closed geodesics associated with pairs of real quadratic embeddings. These formulas include xx-linking and local Eichler-order information [15, Theorems 10 and 63]. More recently, Bruinier–Li–Möller considered all signatures of a pair of quadratic vectors. More specifically, their formula gives the number of pairs of Heegner points at a given distance, and this number is a product of local factors [3, Theorem 1.1(iv) and Corollary 5.9]. We use Bruinier–Li–Möller’s characteristic-matrix and distance formalism, but prove the coarse orbit bound needed here directly from the local structure of quaternion orders and the Bruhat–Tits tree [16, Equation (23.2.4), Theorem 13.3.11, and Sections 23.5.11–23.5.16]. The aggregate estimate provides us with the divisor-sum bound we need for Pasten’s argument.

In the context of the theory of special cycles, Heegner points, and quaternionic Shimura varieties, the natural objects in that framework are CM points [8, 20, 19, 18]. Complete or structured packets satisfy the equidistribution, variance, and small-scale bounds [10, 5, 7, 9, 1, 6, 17]. None of these works states our fixed-radius bound for arbitrary selected submultisets. Our approach is to substitute the classical sup-norm techniques [2, 4] with a local L1L^{1} inequality. Later abc work treats different functionals or additional size hypotheses [13].
