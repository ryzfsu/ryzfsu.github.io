An upper bound for log c in the abc conjecture with exponent 2/9

Ruiyi Zhang Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA
(Date: November 20, 2026)
Abstract.

Put R=rad⁡(a⁢b⁢c)R=\operatorname{rad}(abc) for positive coprime integers a+b=ca+b=c. We obtain log⁡c≪εR2/9+ε\log c\ll_{\varepsilon}R^{2/9+\varepsilon} for every ε>0\varepsilon>0. A large prime power on the side of least radical determines one rational number close to 11 at its prime. Two auxiliary primes give a second, multiplicatively independent number. On their plane a determinant has greater divisibility than its row heights allow.

Key words and phrases:
abc conjecture, radical, logarithmic forms, interpolation determinant

Primary 11D75; Secondary 11J86

1. Introduction

Put R=rad⁡(a⁢b⁢c)=∏p∣a⁢b⁢cpR=\operatorname{rad}(abc)=\prod_{p\mid abc}p. The a⁢b⁢cabc conjecture predicts c≪εR1+εc\ll_{\varepsilon}R^{1+\varepsilon} for positive coprime integers a+b=ca+b=c. The conjecture remains open. Stewart and Yu obtained log⁡c≪R1/3⁢(log⁡R)3\log c\ll R^{1/3}(\log R)^{3} [4, Theorem 1]. The bound in [5, Theorem 1, (1.1)] is log⁡c≪R1/3⁢exp⁡(−log⁡R/(3000⁢log⁡log⁡R))\log c\ll R^{1/3}\exp(-\log R/(3000\log\log R)) for large RR.

Theorem 1.1.

For every real ε>0\varepsilon>0 there exists Cε>0C_{\varepsilon}>0 such that

log⁡c≤Cε⁢R2/9+ε\log c\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{2/9+\varepsilon}

for all coprime positive integers a,ba,b and c=a+bc=a+b.

Write H=log⁡cH=\log c. Equation (1.2) of [5] uses W=log⁡RW=\log R and X=R1/3X=R^{1/3}; here we put x=W/3x=W/3. Order the summands as a≤ba\leq b. For each η\eta as in Section 2 we exclude, for large xx, the range

(1.1) H≥R1/3−η=exp⁡((1−3⁢η)⁢x).H\geq R^{1/3-\eta}=\exp((1-3\eta)x).

The polynomial bound of Section 2 then suffices on bounded xx.

Let ss be a side of least radical. Sections 3 and 4 show that log⁡s\log s is close to HH and that small primes contribute little to it. Since rad⁡(s)≤exp⁡(x)\operatorname{rad}(s)\leq\exp(x), one prime pp carries almost all of HH in vp⁢(s)⁢log⁡pv_{p}(s)\log p (Section 5).

The signed ratio of the other sides is 11 modulo pmp^{m}, where m=vp⁢(s)m=v_{p}(s). Section 6 finds an independent rational number congruent to 11 modulo pp, using two new primes. Put ℓ=log⁡p\ell=\log p. On the plane of their exponents, the height is bounded by g⁢(i,j)=h⁢|i+ξ⁢j|+t⁢|j|g(i,j)=h|i+\xi j|+t|j|, with H−log⁡2≤h≤HH-\log 2\leq h\leq H.

Put ρ=α⁢h⁢t/ℓ\rho=\alpha ht/\ell. The diamond g≤ρg\leq\rho has area N0=2⁢ρ2/(h⁢t)N_{0}=2\rho^{2}/(ht) and its mean height bound is 2⁢ρ/32\rho/3. Section 7 constructs a nonzero minor of order N=2⁢KN=2K from this diamond and a short translate. When N≤3⁢m/2N\leq 3m/2, the divisibility estimate in that section gives

vp⁢(Δ)≥min0≤y≤K,y∈ℝ⁡((K−y)⁢4⁢K/3+y2)=8⁢K2/9=2⁢N2/9.v_{p}(\Delta)\geq\min_{0\leq y\leq K,\;y\in\mathbb{R}}\big{(}(K-y)4K/3+y^{2}% \big{)}=8K^{2}/9=2N^{2}/9.

The minimum occurs at y=2⁢K/3y=2K/3. At the level of areas, 2⁢N0⁢ρ/3<2⁢N02⁢ℓ/92N_{0}\rho/3<2N_{0}^{2}\ell/9 requires α>3/2\alpha>3/2. The depth budget at m=h/ℓm=h/\ell requires α2⁢t/ℓ≤3/4\alpha^{2}t/\ell\leq 3/4. These inequalities allow slack precisely when t/ℓ<1/3t/\ell<1/3. Section 7 obtains t/ℓ<a0t/\ell<a_{0} with 3⁢η<a0<1/33\eta<a_{0}<1/3. Such an interval exists precisely when η<1/9\eta<1/9. Section 8 lets η\eta approach 1/91/9, giving 1/3−1/9=2/91/3-1/9=2/9.
