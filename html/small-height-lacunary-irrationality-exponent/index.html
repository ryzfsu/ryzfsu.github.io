The irrationality exponent of small-height lacunary series

Ruiyi Zhang Independent Researcher, Columbia, Maryland, USA
Abstract.

Let N≥2N\geq 2 be an integer, let u1<u2<⋯u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots be positive integers with lim infuj+1/uj≥2\liminf u_{j+1}/u_{j}\geq 2, and let λj\lambda_{j} be nonzero rational numbers with log⁡H⁢(λj)=o⁢(uj)\log H(\lambda_{j})=o(u_{j}). Then x=∑j≥1λj⁢N−ujx=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}N^{-u_{j}} is irrational and its irrationality exponent is lim supuj+1/uj\limsup u_{j+1}/u_{j}, finite or infinite. The proof compares logarithmic gaps between the continued-fraction denominators of consecutive truncations. A perturbation lemma places each gap of the next truncation in at least one of five classes (bounded, inherited, central, short, reflected), each with an explicit error. Reflection moves a large gap far enough to the right to give a uniform bound at the boundary lim infuj+1/uj=2\liminf u_{j+1}/u_{j}=2. The coefficient condition allows arbitrary signs and cannot be replaced by log⁡H⁢(λj)≤C∗⁢uj\log H(\lambda_{j})\leq C_{*}u_{j} for any fixed C∗>0C_{*}>0.

Key words and phrases:
irrationality exponent, lacunary series, rational height, continued fraction, folding

1. Introduction

For an irrational real number ξ\xi, its irrationality exponent is

μ⁢(ξ)=sup{ν∈ℝ:|ξ−pq|<q−ν⁢ for infinitely many ⁢(p,q)∈ℤ×ℤ>0}.\mu(\xi)=\sup\left\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}:\left|\xi-\frac{p}{q}\right|<q^{-\nu}% \text{ for infinitely many }(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\right\}.

For a nonzero rational number written in lowest terms, put

(1.1) H⁢(a/b)=max⁡(|a|,b),a∈ℤ∖{0},b∈ℤ>0,gcd⁡(a,b)=1.H(a/b)=\max(|a|,b),\qquad a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\quad b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>% 0},\quad\gcd(a,b)=1.

For y∈ℚy\in\mathbb{Q}, let den⁡(y)\operatorname{den}(y) denote its positive reduced denominator. All logarithms are natural.

Theorem 1.

Let N≥2N\geq 2 be an integer, let u1<u2<⋯u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots be positive integers, and let λj∈ℚ∖{0}\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\} satisfy

log⁡H⁢(λj)=o⁢(uj),\log H(\lambda_{j})=o(u_{j}),

and suppose that

lim infj→∞uj+1uj≥2.\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{u_{j+1}}{u_{j}}\geq 2.

Then the absolutely convergent series

x=∑j≥1λj⁢N−ujx=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}N^{-u_{j}}

is irrational and

(1.2) μ⁢(x)=lim supj→∞uj+1uj.\mu(x)=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{u_{j+1}}{u_{j}}.

Both sides lie in [2,+∞][2,+\infty].

The ratios need not converge. For example, integer exponent sequences with repeating ratio patterns (2,2,3)(2,2,3) and (2,5/2)(2,5/2) give exponents 33 and 5/25/2, respectively. Ratios may fall below 22 by a quantity tending to zero. The coefficients may have either sign; any sequence from a finite subset of ℚ∖{0}\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\} is allowed. The binary specialization uj=2j−1u_{j}=2^{j-1} of Theorem 1 is the main theorem of [10], proved there with Hankel determinants and Padé approximants; the present proof uses a different argument based on regular continued fractions, and the sharpness examples of Section 9 follow Section 6 of that paper.

For an integer base b≥2b\geq 2 and a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers (nj)(n_{j}) with nj+1/nj≥2n_{j+1}/n_{j}\geq 2 for all large jj, the formula μ⁢(∑jb−nj)=lim supnj+1/nj\mu(\sum_{j}b^{-n_{j}})=\limsup n_{j+1}/n_{j} for unit coefficients is a classical consequence of folding, recorded in Bugeaud, Krieger and Shallit [4] with attribution there to Bugeaud [2]. Bugeaud [3, Theorem 7.21] gives a result on prescribed irrationality exponents for a specific family in the middle-third Cantor set. The explicit Cantor-set family in Bugeaud [2, Theorem 2] consists, up to a fixed rational factor, of special cases of the unit-weight part of Theorem 1. Theorem 1 also treats small rational weights and permits ratios tending to 22 from below. Continued-fraction expansions of lacunary series appear in Shallit [8, 9]. The reversal used here is related to the folding identities of Mendès France [6] and van der Poorten and Shallit [7].

Duverney, Kurosawa and Shiokawa [5, Theorem 1] give an irrationality-exponent formula for series ∑jσj/Xj\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}/X_{j}, where σj∈{−1,1}\sigma_{j}\in\{-1,1\}, Xj>1X_{j}>1 are rational, and Xj+1≥Xj2X_{j+1}\geq X_{j}^{2} eventually. Write TjT_{j} for their auxiliary sequence of positive integers, defined by

(1.3) T1=den⁡(X1),Tj+1=Tj2⁢den⁡(Xj+1/Xj2).T_{1}=\operatorname{den}(X_{1}),\qquad T_{j+1}=T_{j}^{2}\operatorname{den}(X_{% j+1}/X_{j}^{2}).

The additional hypothesis is log⁡Tj+1=o⁢(log⁡Xj)\log T_{j+1}=o(\log X_{j}). Their conclusion is

(1.4) μ⁢(∑jσjXj)=lim supjlog⁡Xj+1log⁡Xj.\mu\!\left(\sum_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}}{X_{j}}\right)=\limsup_{j}\frac{\log X_{j+% 1}}{\log X_{j}}.

For λj∈{−1,1}\lambda_{j}\in\{-1,1\} and uj+1≥2⁢uju_{j+1}\geq 2u_{j} eventually, discard a finite prefix and take Xj=NujX_{j}=N^{u_{j}}. Then Xj+1/Xj2X_{j+1}/X_{j}^{2} is an integer and Tj=1T_{j}=1, so the DKS formula gives (1.2), including the case of exact ratio 22.

For the following comparison with arbitrary rational weights, assumelim supuj+1/uj<∞\limsup u_{j+1}/u_{j}<\infty, so that uj+1=O⁢(uj)u_{j+1}=O(u_{j}). In the subcase lim infuj+1/uj>2\liminf u_{j+1}/u_{j}>2, discard finitely many terms and take Xj=Nuj/|λj|X_{j}=N^{u_{j}}/|\lambda_{j}| and σj=sgn⁡(λj)\sigma_{j}=\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda_{j}). The height hypothesis makes log⁡Xj\log X_{j} asymptotic to uj⁢log⁡Nu_{j}\log N, so Xj>1X_{j}>1 eventually and the logarithmic ratios have the same limsup as the exponent ratios. The strict lower-ratio margin then gives Xj+1≥Xj2X_{j+1}\geq X_{j}^{2} eventually. The local logarithmic denominator costs are o⁢(uj)o(u_{j}): if λj=αj/βj\lambda_{j}=\alpha_{j}/\beta_{j} in lowest terms, then

den⁡(Xj+1/Xj2)≤|αj+1|⁢βj2.\operatorname{den}(X_{j+1}/X_{j}^{2})\leq|\alpha_{j+1}|\beta_{j}^{2}.

Put hj=log⁡H⁢(λj)/ujh_{j}=\log H(\lambda_{j})/u_{j}, so hj→0h_{j}\to 0. Since uj+1≥(2+ε∗)⁢uju_{j+1}\geq(2+\varepsilon_{*})u_{j} eventually for some ε∗>0\varepsilon_{*}>0, the fixed initial denominator cost is negligible after normalization, and the normalized later costs are bounded by a convergent geometric sum. Their accumulation with weights 2j−i2^{j-i}, i≤ji\leq j, is therefore still o⁢(uj)o(u_{j}), giving log⁡Tj+1=o⁢(log⁡Xj)\log T_{j+1}=o(\log X_{j}) as required by DKS. Adding back the omitted rational prefix does not change the irrationality exponent. The case of an infinite limsup follows from the truncations alone (Section 5). Amou and Bugeaud [1, Lemma 3] give a related criterion. In notation local to this paragraph, let A^n\widehat{A}_{n} be strictly increasing positive integers, let B^n\widehat{B}_{n} be nonzero integers, and let τ^=lim suplog⁡A^n+1/log⁡A^n\widehat{\tau}=\limsup\log\widehat{A}_{n+1}/\log\widehat{A}_{n} be finite and at least two. Their local condition is

A^n+1/|B^n+1|(A^n/|B^n|)2≥2eventually,\frac{\widehat{A}_{n+1}/|\widehat{B}_{n+1}|}{(\widehat{A}_{n}/|\widehat{B}_{n}% |)^{2}}\geq 2\quad\text{eventually},

where S^n=(A^n+1/|B^n+1|)/(A^n/|B^n|)2\widehat{S}_{n}=(\widehat{A}_{n+1}/|\widehat{B}_{n+1}|)/(\widehat{A}_{n}/|% \widehat{B}_{n}|)^{2} denotes the left-hand side. Let d^n=den⁡(S^n)\widehat{d}_{n}=\operatorname{den}(\widehat{S}_{n}), d^0=|B^1|\widehat{d}_{0}=|\widehat{B}_{1}|, and

D^n=∏i=0nd^i2n−i.\widehat{D}_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}\widehat{d}_{i}^{2^{n-i}}.

Their remaining conditions are |B^n|=A^no⁢(1)|\widehat{B}_{n}|=\widehat{A}_{n}^{o(1)} and D^n=A^no⁢(1)\widehat{D}_{n}=\widehat{A}_{n}^{o(1)}. Under these conditions, the irrationality exponent of the series with terms B^n/A^n\widehat{B}_{n}/\widehat{A}_{n} equals τ^\widehat{\tau}. In the proof of their Theorem 2, Amou and Bugeaud [1, Section 5] state that there exists a sequence of ratios greater than 22 with lower limit 22 for which Lemma 3 applies to their construction; the omitted details concern the cumulative denominator condition D^n=A^no⁢(1)\widehat{D}_{n}=\widehat{A}_{n}^{o(1)}.

At the boundary lim infuj+1/uj=2\liminf u_{j+1}/u_{j}=2, for the choice Xj=Nuj/|λj|X_{j}=N^{u_{j}}/|\lambda_{j}| above, the height hypothesis alone does not imply log⁡Tj+1=o⁢(log⁡Xj)\log T_{j+1}=o(\log X_{j}): weighting local costs by 2j−i2^{j-i} can leave a nonnegligible contribution. Also, with this choice, a step uj+1<2⁢uju_{j+1}<2u_{j} gives Xj+1<Xj2X_{j+1}<X_{j}^{2} for unit coefficients. Theorem 1 includes arbitrary rational weights of the stated height at this boundary and permits such steps below 22.

The proof uses only regular continued fractions and rational arithmetic. The continued fraction of one truncation need not be a prefix of that of the next, so a perturbation lemma controls each gap separately. The reflection estimate gives a lower bound for the rightward displacement of a large gap in terms of its old width. At the boundary ratio 22 there is no margin, and this shift takes its place. For the infinite continued fraction, each gap gets its own truncation, chosen so that the accumulated finite-stage error stays logarithmic.

2. Continued-fraction facts

For a nonintegral rational number we use its canonical finite regular continued fraction, whose last partial quotient is at least 22. For an integer, the canonical expansion consists of that integer alone. For irrational numbers we use infinite regular continued fractions. Convergents are indexed from zero and written with positive denominators; the auxiliary pair with denominator zero is not counted as a convergent. A convergent preceding the terminal rational is called nonterminal.

If p/qp/q is a nonterminal convergent of a real number θ\theta and the next convergent has denominator q+q^{+}, then

(2.1) 1q⁢(q++q)≤|θ−pq|≤1q⁢q+.\frac{1}{q(q^{+}+q)}\leq\left|\theta-\frac{p}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{qq^{+}}.

They also hold for rational θ\theta with its canonical finite expansion. We use Legendre’s criterion in both cases: if p/qp/q is reduced and

(2.2) |θ−pq|<12⁢q2,\left|\theta-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{2q^{2}},

then p/qp/q is a convergent of θ\theta. If θ\theta is rational and the error is nonzero, it is nonterminal. For a nonintegral rational θ\theta, let qm−1q_{m-1} and qmq_{m} be the penultimate and final denominators in its canonical expansion. The strict inequality rules out the penultimate convergent of the alternative expansion ending in 11: its denominator is qm−qm−1q_{m}-q_{m-1} and its error is 1/(qm⁢(qm−qm−1))1/(q_{m}(q_{m}-q_{m-1})), so the strict threshold cannot hold when the canonical last partial quotient is at least 22. For integral θ\theta, a distinct rational with positive denominator has error at least the reciprocal of that denominator, so the strict criterion can hold only at θ\theta itself. For irrational θ\theta every convergent is nonterminal.

A logarithmic gap is a pair a=log⁡qa=\log q, b=log⁡q+b=\log q^{+} from consecutive convergent denominators; its width is g=b−ag=b-a. A possible initial equality q=q+=1q=q^{+}=1 is allowed.

5. Arithmetic of the truncations

Write λj=αj/βj\lambda_{j}=\alpha_{j}/\beta_{j} in reduced form with βj>0\beta_{j}>0, and put

(5.1) tn=un⁢log⁡N,xn=∑j≤nλj⁢N−uj=PnVnt_{n}=u_{n}\log N,\qquad x_{n}=\sum_{j\leq n}\lambda_{j}N^{-u_{j}}=\frac{P_{n}% }{V_{n}}

in reduced form with Vn>0V_{n}>0. The ratio assumption gives a fixed lower ratio ρ>1\rho>1 for all large indices, for example ρ=3/2\rho=3/2. The height hypothesis and geometric growth imply

(5.2) ∑j≤nlog⁡H⁢(λj)=o⁢(un).\sum_{j\leq n}\log H(\lambda_{j})=o(u_{n}).

8. Completion of the proof

For an irrational xx with convergent denominators qmq_{m}, (2.1) gives

−log⁡|x−pm/qm|log⁡qm=1+log⁡qm+1log⁡qm+o⁢(1).-\frac{\log|x-p_{m}/q_{m}|}{\log q_{m}}=1+\frac{\log q_{m+1}}{\log q_{m}}+o(1).

By Legendre’s criterion every reduced approximation with |x−p/q|<1/(2⁢q2)|x-p/q|<1/(2q^{2}) is a convergent; so is every reduced approximation of fixed exponent greater than 22 once qq is large. In the definition of μ\mu, a pair (p,q)(p,q) need not be reduced. Reducing it does not increase the denominator, so the same inequality holds. A fixed reduced fraction occurs only finitely often among approximations of a fixed positive exponent, because its error is nonzero. In a bounded interval there are only finitely many reduced fractions with bounded denominator. Thus infinitely many such approximations have unbounded reduced denominators, and the preceding Legendre argument applies when the exponent is greater than 22. Therefore

(8.1) μ⁢(x)=1+lim supmlog⁡qm+1log⁡qm=2+lim supmlog⁡(qm+1/qm)log⁡qm.\mu(x)=1+\limsup_{m}\frac{\log q_{m+1}}{\log q_{m}}=2+\limsup_{m}\frac{\log(q_% {m+1}/q_{m})}{\log q_{m}}.

9. Height sharpness and scope

The coefficient condition cannot be replaced by log⁡H⁢(λj)≤C∗⁢uj\log H(\lambda_{j})\leq C_{*}u_{j} with any prescribed constant C∗>0C_{*}>0. The first construction below shows this. The second shows that, at one fixed linear height scale, the sum can be rational or have infinite irrationality exponent. In this section index the exponents by j≥0j\geq 0 and take uj=2ju_{j}=2^{j}. Write ℓ=log⁡N\ell=\log N.

Fix 0<θ<1/30<\theta<1/3, and set

mj=⌊θ⁢2j⌋,λj=N(−1)j⁢mj,ej=2j−(−1)j⁢mj.m_{j}=\lfloor\theta 2^{j}\rfloor,\qquad\lambda_{j}=N^{(-1)^{j}m_{j}},\qquad e_% {j}=2^{j}-(-1)^{j}m_{j}.

These positive rational weights give

log⁡H⁢(λj)=mj⁢ℓ=θ⁢ℓ⁢2j+O⁢(1),x=∑j≥0N−ej.\log H(\lambda_{j})=m_{j}\ell=\theta\ell 2^{j}+O(1),\qquad x=\sum_{j\geq 0}N^{% -e_{j}}.

The exponents eje_{j} are strictly increasing. For odd jj their difference is at least (1−3⁢θ)⁢2j>0(1-3\theta)2^{j}>0; for even jj it is 2j+mj+mj+1>02^{j}+m_{j}+m_{j+1}>0. Their gaps tend to infinity, so the base-NN expansion is not eventually periodic and xx is irrational. The truncation pj/qjp_{j}/q_{j} through index jj has reduced denominator qj=Nejq_{j}=N^{e_{j}}, since its numerator is 11 modulo NN, and

0<x−pj/qj≤N−ej+11−N−1≤2⁢N−ej+1.0<x-p_{j}/q_{j}\leq\frac{N^{-e_{j+1}}}{1-N^{-1}}\leq 2N^{-e_{j+1}}.

Along even indices ej+1/ej→2⁢(1+θ)/(1−θ)e_{j+1}/e_{j}\to 2(1+\theta)/(1-\theta), whence

(9.1) μ⁢(x)≥2⁢(1+θ)1−θ>2.\mu(x)\geq\frac{2(1+\theta)}{1-\theta}>2.

Choosing 0<θ<min⁡{1/3,C∗/ℓ}0<\theta<\min\{1/3,C_{*}/\ell\} makes log⁡H⁢(λj)≤C∗⁢2j\log H(\lambda_{j})\leq C_{*}2^{j} for every jj.

At the fixed linear height scale below, positive integer weights can give a rational sum. Set

λ0=N−1,λj=N2j−1−1(j≥1).\lambda_{0}=N-1,\qquad\lambda_{j}=N^{2^{j-1}}-1\quad(j\geq 1).

Then λj⁢N−2j=N−2j−1−N−2j\lambda_{j}N^{-2^{j}}=N^{-2^{j-1}}-N^{-2^{j}} for j≥1j\geq 1, so

∑j=0mλj⁢N−2j=1−N−2m⟶1,log⁡H⁢(λj)∼12⁢ℓ⁢2j.\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda_{j}N^{-2^{j}}=1-N^{-2^{m}}\longrightarrow 1,\qquad\log H% (\lambda_{j})\sim\tfrac{1}{2}\ell 2^{j}.

At the same scale, replace the later weights by

λ~j=N2j−1−1+𝟏{j=r2⁢ for some ⁢r≥1}(j≥1),λ~0=N−1.\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}=N^{2^{j-1}}-1+\boldsymbol{1}_{\{j=r^{2}\text{ for some% }r\geq 1\}}\quad(j\geq 1),\qquad\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}=N-1.

They remain positive integers, with logarithmic height asymptotic to 12⁢ℓ⁢2j\tfrac{1}{2}\ell 2^{j}. Their sum is

x~=1+ξN,ξN=∑r≥1N−2r2.\widetilde{x}=1+\xi_{N},\qquad\xi_{N}=\sum_{r\geq 1}N^{-2^{r^{2}}}.

The digit expansion shows that ξN\xi_{N} is irrational. The truncation through r=Jr=J has reduced denominator qJ=N2J2q_{J}=N^{2^{J^{2}}}, since its numerator is one modulo NN, and error

0<ξN−∑r=1JN−2r2≤2⁢N−2(J+1)2=2⁢qJ−22⁢J+1.0<\xi_{N}-\sum_{r=1}^{J}N^{-2^{r^{2}}}\leq 2N^{-2^{(J+1)^{2}}}=2q_{J}^{-2^{2J+% 1}}.

Adding one to each approximant preserves its reduced denominator and its approximation error. Thus μ⁢(x~)=+∞\mu(\widetilde{x})=+\infty.

The height condition is sufficient but not necessary for a given series. For example, if b≥2b\geq 2 is an integer, the weights λj=b−2j\lambda_{j}=b^{-2^{j}} have logarithmic height (log⁡b)⁢2j(\log b)2^{j}, but their sum is ∑j≥0(N⁢b)−2j\sum_{j\geq 0}(Nb)^{-2^{j}}, which has exponent 22 by Theorem 1 with base N⁢bNb and unit weights.

Theorem 1 does not cover lim infuj+1/uj<2\liminf u_{j+1}/u_{j}<2. The examples above keep the exponent ratios equal to 22 and vary only the coefficient heights. Problem 10.40 in [3, p. 220] asks for the irrationality exponent of binary lacunary sums ∑j2−nj\sum_{j}2^{-n_{j}} for an arbitrary increasing lacunary exponent sequence (nj)(n_{j}). For N=2N=2, Theorem 1 gives the exponent when lim infnj+1/nj≥2\liminf n_{j+1}/n_{j}\geq 2, including ratios tending to 22 from below, and allows small rational weights. The case with eventual ratio at least 22 is recorded in [4] for unit coefficients, with attribution there to Bugeaud’s earlier work, and for coefficients ±1\pm 1 it also follows from [5, Theorem 1]. Under the same height hypothesis, the subcase 2<lim infnj+1/nj≤lim supnj+1/nj<∞2<\liminf n_{j+1}/n_{j}\leq\limsup n_{j+1}/n_{j}<\infty also follows from [5, Theorem 1].
